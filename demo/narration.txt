Phishing succeeds when a message pressures someone to act before they can check it. PhishLens is a calm layer between a suspicious message and a risky click.

Here, a student can paste or choose a message and inspect the sender, claimed organization, and channel. The analysis makes the pressure visible: urgency, a request for credentials, impersonation cues, secrecy, and a destination that does not match the claimed organization. The link is parsed locally, never opened.

PhishLens shows observed factors, not a misleading confidence percentage. It then gives a practical next move: use an official route, ask a trusted adult or school contact, and save a redacted local report if needed.

For a second perspective, the product can ask a local Ollama model for constrained defensive triage. The deterministic evidence engine still works when that model is unavailable.

Finally, the interface exposes a 24-case safety regression check. It is a transparent behavior check, not a claim of real-world model accuracy. PhishLens helps people slow down, understand the pressure, and take a safer next step.
