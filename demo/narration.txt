Phishing succeeds when a message pressures someone to act before they can check it. PhishLens is a calm layer between a suspicious message and a risky click.

A student can paste or choose a message and inspect the sender, claimed organization, and channel. The analysis makes the pressure visible: urgency, a request for credentials, impersonation cues, secrecy, and a destination that does not match the claimed organization. Links are parsed locally and never opened.

The advanced evidence panel handles harder cases without sending the content anywhere. Users can paste email authentication headers, preview a screenshot, decode a QR value when the browser supports it, or supply visible screenshot text. PhishLens surfaces encoded destinations, unusual ports, look-alike domains, and the limits of reported SPF, DKIM, and DMARC results.

PhishLens shows observed factors, not a misleading confidence percentage. It gives a practical next move: use an official route, ask a trusted adult or school contact, and save a redacted local report if needed.

For a second perspective, the product can ask a local Ollama model for constrained defensive triage. The comparison panel makes shared, engine-only, and AI-only signals visible. The deterministic evidence engine still works when that model is unavailable.

The 36-case safety dataset includes benign, multilingual, misspelled, QR and image-text, header, and adversarial behavior checks. It reports transparent metrics for this synthetic set, not real-world accuracy. A consent-first handoff, accessibility controls, and a local ethical pilot keep the workflow practical and honest.

Run 90-second judge mode to replay the proof: evidence, a redacted safe report, and behavior metrics. PhishLens helps people slow down, understand the pressure, and take a safer next step.
